SecChain keeps the secrets of your development projects in the Keychain instead of .env files, and hands them to the commands that need them without ever printing them.

On your Mac, the secchain command-line tool stores an API key or a token per repository and passes it to a command as an environment variable. On this app, the same secrets are with you on your iPhone and iPad: you can see what a project needs, add a secret while you are away from your Mac, and reveal a value after Face ID or Touch ID. This app also answers for your Mac: when a command there needs a protected secret, it shows what is being asked and approves it after Face ID.

WHAT IT DOES

- Secrets belong to a repository, so the same name can hold a different value in each project.
- Values live in the Keychain of your devices and nowhere else. They are never written to a file, a log, or a server.
- Secrets you mark for iCloud Keychain follow you between your devices, end-to-end encrypted by Apple. Secrets you keep on one device stay there.
- A value is hidden until you ask for it, and revealing one always requires authentication.
- A protection level per secret decides when authentication is required: never for a routine command, every time it is read, or enforced by the Keychain itself.
- A Mac paired with this iPhone can ask you to approve a command that reads a protected secret. You see which Mac asks, the repository, the secret names, and the command, and Face ID approves it — for a Mac without Touch ID, for an SSH session, and for commands an AI coding agent starts while you are away.

PRIVACY

SecChain collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, and no advertising. The developer cannot see your secrets, their names, or which projects you work on. An approval request travels through your own iCloud account, never carries a secret value, and the developer cannot read it either.

SecChain is open source under the MIT License. The macOS app and the command-line tool are available separately from the project's site.
